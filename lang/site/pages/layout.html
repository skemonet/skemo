Layout and positions

skemo places the cards; you choose a direction, and pin only what you care about.

Direction

dir="down" (default)

dir="right"

diagram(dir="…") sets the main direction of the arrows: down (default), right, up or left. right suits pipelines and processes, down hierarchies and networks. Cards are arranged in layers along that direction and aligned in columns inside zones. Cards of the same row keep the order you wrote them in, inside zones too; an arrow written after its target still points in the main direction.

Tidy layers (preview)

A card sits centred over the cards it leads to when it is their only parent, or over the card in its column. Arrows leave and enter in the direction of the diagram, rows inside a zone are as far apart as outside, and two arrows never share a line where they meet a card.

Rows in a zone (preview)

wrap="N" on a zone puts at most N children per row (per column with dir="right"), on a grid, in writing order. Useful when a zone of many unconnected cards would otherwise make one very long row; (preview) a zone of four cards or more that have no arrow at all (an inventory) is laid as such a grid by itself, about as many columns as rows. When a card ends up behind another as seen from the card it is linked to, a warning says so; not with wrap="1", a single file of cards (a server and its stack) whose arrows come in by the free sides. When an arrow from outside reaches a card beyond the first row, the zone's rows (and those of the zones beside it) are spread to leave a corridor between them, which other arrows may cross, so that the line does not go round the zone.

Stacks (preview)

With wrap="1", a zone is a stack: its cards share one width, the widest one's, on a common left edge. Sibling zones side by side start on the same line.

Layers of the same width (preview)

diagram(zones="stretch") gives zones stacked along the direction the width of the widest one, like layers drawn by hand. Only the frames grow: no card moves, no number is written in the file.

Groups in order: stack (preview)

A stack draws nothing: it lays its children, zones or cards, one after the other in writing order along the direction (here pods, services, nodes, then the network), whatever the arrows between them. Arrows still align what they link (each service sits under its pods); one going back up the stack leaves by the top and enters by the bottom. A .bare zone without a title groups cards without drawing anything (the three nodes). A card belongs to one group only: zones and stacks form a tree. The zones of a stack share one width, like layers. A card of a zone whose arrows all leave the zone, with none coming in, sits on the zone's first row. Classes .col-w1, .col-w2… line up each service and its pods over the worker that hosts them, across zones; the workers' .wide zone spreads them over the full width and gives the columns their place: each worker is as wide as the widest group of its column, so two pods never straddle two workers.

Target box (preview)

diagram(fit="16:9") states where the diagram will be shown (a slide; 8:9 for half a slide), or its size in pixels: fit="1280x720". What matters is how large the text ends up once the diagram is fitted in that box, not only its proportions: a layout with the right ratio but twice as big shows smaller text. skemo never changes the layout by itself for it, so the drawing stays stable while you edit: when a setting such as zone#ns(wrap="3") or diagram(dir="right") would show the text at least 15 % larger, it warns and names it (with a box in pixels, the text size before and after). skemo fit f.skemo lists the candidates, each one laid out for real, with the gain in text size, and --write puts the best one in the file. When the proportions are more than 15 % off the target and no setting helps, the warning says so: the layout itself has to change.

Pinned positions

at="x,y" pins the top-left corner of a card or zone, in pixels. Cards without at keep their automatic place; every arrow is routed either way. The graphical editor writes at= for you when you drag a card.

Adjusting an arrow

side="start-end" forces the sides an arrow leaves and enters by; via="x,y" makes it pass through waypoints. In the example above, fw → web leaves by the left side and fw → db passes through 440,200.

Stability

Editing one card only moves the arrows attached to it: positions do not shift around when you add a line or rename a title. Pinning a card exactly where the layout put it changes nothing in the drawing. This is what makes text and drag-and-drop editing safe to mix.

Overlaps

A card pinned on top of another is never moved: the file shows exactly what you wrote. skemo emits a warning that names both cards instead, and the editor offers to separate them.

In timeline, frieze and mind map modes, positions come from the dates or from the tree: at= has no effect there (warning).
